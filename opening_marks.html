Opening Marks.cs · 1983 lines

Select a floor plan view, press Scan, tick what you want, press Mark. The macro finds every opening and recess in the view and crosses it out: two diagonals for an opening, one for a recess. That includes the holes no cut makes, like a stair hole left between three planks and a cast-in-place strip.

A

What it does

It runs inside the drawing editor, on the view you have selected, and writes lines and dimensions straight into the drawing. The window stays open over the drawing, with two tabs: Openings, which lists what the scan found, and Results, a log of every run in the session. A run goes through four stages.

  1. 01

    Scan the view

    Nothing is read until Scan is pressed. Every part shown in the view is read, not only main parts, as long as it lies at least partly inside the view's depth. Openings are found two ways, from the silhouette of the view and from the parts' cuts, and each one gets one of five types.

  2. 02

    Review the list

    The openings are listed under their type. Click a type's name to expand it to its openings, and again to collapse it. Each opening has a MARK tick for the crossing and a DIMS tick for its size, and a type's ticks set the whole group. Marks are ticked by default, dimensions only when asked for. Selecting rows outlines those openings in the drawing with a temporary magenta rectangle, so it is always clear which one a row is.

  3. 03

    Mark

    Every opening ticked under MARK gets two diagonals fitted to its real shape, and a recess gets one diagonal over its box. Every opening ticked under DIMS gets its width along the top edge and its height along the left edge. Earlier marks are kept, and a line or dimension already in the same place is not drawn again.

  4. 04

    Remove, when needed

    Every line and dimension the macro places carries a user property, MCP_OPEN_MARK, with its type. Remove takes off the macro's own lines of the types ticked under MARK and its dimensions of the types ticked under DIMS, and nothing else in the drawing.

What the Results tab reports

Parts read · openings marked, per type · lines drawn · dimensions added · failed to insert · already there, not redrawn. If a mark could not be tagged, it warns that Remove will not find it.

B

The five types

The type decides how an opening is listed and which group's ticks it follows. It also lets Remove take off one kind of mark without touching the others.

C

Finding an opening that no cut makes

Tekla draws each element's outline but has no notion of "the opening". Many openings on a precast floor are not cuts at all. A stair hole between three planks and a cast-in-place strip has no cut in any element, only a space where no element is.

So the view is rasterized into a silhouette. Every face of every part in the view's depth is filled into a bitmap, each part in its own colour, so every empty pixel also knows which parts are next to it. The cell is 5 mm, and grows only when the view is so large that the bitmap would pass 2500 pixels on its longer side. Material is then grown by half the joint limit, which closes the plank joints, and every empty region that cannot be reached from the border is an enclosed opening. Without that closing, one 20 mm joint would connect a stair hole to the outside and hide it.

The cuts are read as well, for what the silhouette cannot enclose: notches open to the outside, and holes narrower than the joint limit, which the closing fills in. A ray cast along the viewing direction through the middle of each cut tells a through opening (no material) from a recess (material behind it) from a cut that something else fills.

An enclosed opening counts as Edge / corner when the cuts of the flat elements around it cover at least half of its area. Otherwise it is Between elements.

D

The measuring

The bitmap only says where an opening is. Its edges are measured on the real solids: rays are cast across each edge on three lines, and the change from empty to material is found by bisection. The cell size limits what is found, not how exactly it is measured.

The first version snapped each edge to the nearest corner of a part around it instead. A check on a live view found edges from a few millimetres to about 36 mm off the real boundary. A hollow-core plank has many corners close together around its cores, and the snap picked the wrong one.

The lone point

The rays hid a trap of their own. Through a cut hole in a hollow-core plank, Tekla's Solid.Intersect returns one point, on the plank's top face plane, where there is no material at all. A ray that starts and ends outside a part crosses its surface an even number of times, so a lone point is now read as empty. Before that, every hollow-core hole looked solid, its edges were never measured, and the same trap made cut openings look filled and dropped them.

After both fixes, every line end of the 37 openings marked on the test view lay within 1 mm of the real edge.

Round openings

A round opening is crossed inside its circle. Each half-diagonal steps out from the centre in 20 mm steps and stops at the first material of a flat element. Stepping matters: bisecting the whole half-diagonal landed on a later edge when the line crossed a wall and came out into empty space.

E

Telling the floor from what stands in it

A plan view's depth reaches below the slab, so walls, columns and their corbels are in the view too. Their cuts are not openings of the floor. The pockets at a wall head showed up as 85 × 140 mm "edge openings".

An element is now treated as standing when it is taller along the viewing direction than it is wide and reaches out of the view's depth. Both are needed: the cast-in-place strips between planks are narrower than their 265 mm thickness, but lie within the floor. On the test view 1,288 parts are flat and 284 stand.

Standing elements still fill the silhouette, so they still close the gaps they stand in. But they have no openings of their own, stop no crossing, and their door and pocket cuts do not decide an opening's type.

The tie-bar cores

The hollow cores opened at plank ends for tie bars are recesses to the geometry. On the test view there were 846 of them, 100 to 150 mm wide. A filter on cut class would depend on how each model names them, so the recess limit is a size instead: below 200 mm on the narrower side, a recess is ignored. That leaves 0 of them on the test view.

H

Limitations

  • Walls under a slab hole can split it structural

    Standing walls still fill the silhouette, so a stair hole with a room of walls below it comes out as two openings. Dropping walls that do not reach the slab layer was tried and made many openings wrong, so it was taken out.

  • A recess keeps its cut box quality

    There is material under a recess everywhere, so the rays cannot find its edges. It keeps its cut box, clamped to the element. A cutting part larger than the pocket makes the line too long, and a recess cut across two elements shows as two halves.

  • Dimension placement is unchecked open

    Dimensions sit a fixed 6 mm from the edge on paper, width on top and height on the left. Openings close together will overlap theirs, and their placement has not been checked on a drawing.

  • Recesses unchecked on a real view open

    The test view's only recesses were the tie-bar cores, which the recess limit removes. Recess marking has not yet been checked on a view that has real ones.

  • Edge / corner is a judgement quality

    An opening counts as Edge / corner when flat-element cuts cover at least half of it. The half was set on the stair holes of one model.

  • Remove works by type, not by opening workflow

    One opening's marks are removed by hand.

  • An old tag can outlive a type change workflow

    Mark skips a line already drawn in the same place without looking at the old line's type. After an opening's type changes, the old tag stays until Remove is run for that type.

  • Select the view again before scanning workflow

    After rows are selected, the view is no longer selected in Tekla, and has to be selected again before the next Scan.

  • Loaded once per Tekla session process

    Editing a deployed .cs makes Tekla recompile it, but the assembly already in memory keeps running. Restart Tekla after updating the macro.

J

Running it

  1. Copy the macro into the drawings subfolder of XS_MACRO_DIRECTORY, then restart Tekla. Macros are registered only at startup.
  2. Open the drawing and select exactly one view, a floor plan.
  3. Run Opening Marks from Applications & components.
  4. Set Min, Joint and Recess if the defaults do not suit the model, then press Scan.
  5. Expand the types, check the openings by selecting rows, and untick any you do not want. Tick DIMS where you want sizes.
  6. Set the colour and line type, then press Mark. The status line and the Results tab show what was drawn.
  7. To undo the macro's marks of one type, tick that type and press Remove.
